Can a data business become the starting point for a permanent capital vehicle, rather than the entirety of what that vehicle owns?

The distinction matters. The useful life of one dataset, customer relationship, or product may be shorter than the useful life of the capital it generates. A business could serve its customers well today while funding something that compounds over a much longer period.

Calling this “data as float” leaves an important question unresolved: what exactly is the source of funding? Revenue, cash flow, and float are different things. The structure needs to work without depending on the label.

A few questions I’m working through:

- **Durability:** How much ongoing investment does the data business need to remain useful as customer needs and technology change?
- **Available capital:** How much cash can it actually redeploy after meeting its obligations and reinvesting in the original business?
- **Ownership:** What capital structure allows that cash to support long-term ownership of other businesses?
- **Discipline:** What makes the next investment better than reinvesting in the existing business or returning capital?

The interesting possibility is a company whose first business creates room to build or own others. The risk is treating a temporary source of cash as a permanent advantage.

I’d be interested in examples where the economics and ownership structure make this work—and examples where they don’t.
